      How Uplink works

      A business photographs leftover food. AI identifies it. Three agents with competing goals negotiate where it goes, with no boss. Code removes unsafe plans first, and when the agents can't or shouldn't agree they ask a person.

      1. 1
        Photo

        The business takes one photo (or a camera spots food at closing time). A vision model lists the items, servings, allergens and how long it stays safe. The business fixes anything wrong.

      2. 2
        Safety rules

        Plain code removes every unsafe shelter and driver pair: wrong food type, no fridge, allergen risk, no room, closed, or arriving too late. Agents never see these plans and cannot override the rules.

      3. 3
        Three agents negotiate

        Speed, Cost and Need each propose a plan and vote on every plan across up to three rounds. Shared code only counts the votes. There is no supervisor.

      4. 4
        Outcome, or a refusal to agree

        Consensus, or a majority with the dissent kept on record. If no plan has a majority, the winner crosses a red line, the plan is over budget, or a fact is unconfirmed, the agents stop and ask a person.

      5. 5
        Delivery and record

        A driver delivers the food and a California SB 1383 donation record is created automatically.

      The three agents

      AgentGoalRed line (a veto)
      SpeedKeep a big safety margin before the food spoils, weighted by how reliable the driver is.Under 10 minutes to spare, or a driver under 50% reliable.
      CostSpend as little as possible. A no-show means paying for a rescue, so reliability counts.More than $20 for one donation.
      NeedFeed whoever has waited longest and has room.Feeding a shelter that got food today while another reachable shelter has waited 4+ days.

      Words you will see

      Red line / veto
      One line an agent will not cross. A veto blocks a plan in every round, however many others like it.
      Consensus
      All three agents accept the same plan.
      Majority with dissent
      Two agents accept and none vetoes. The third agent's objection stays on record.
      Unacceptable compromise
      The majority's plan crosses the third agent's red line, so a human decides.
      Irreconcilable
      After 3 rounds no plan has majority support, so a human decides.
      Insufficient evidence
      The decision hinges on an unconfirmed fact (usually allergens), so the agents ask the business instead of guessing.
      Fallback rule
      If nobody answers in time, the agents' top-ranked option goes and the feed says so. It exists so food doesn't spoil while waiting.

      What is real and what is simulated

      Real

      • The negotiation: proposals, votes, objections, vetoes and fallbacks.
      • The safety rules, enforced in code.
      • Photo reading (a vision model), and camera detection when a camera is connected.
      • Shelter answers from a person on a phone.

      Simulated

      • The city, the shelters and the drivers.
      • The clock: one minute is one second at 1× speed.
      • Driver movement along straight lines.
      • Events such as a full shelter, a no-show or a new pantry (triggered from Demo controls).

      More detail: README.md and docs/ARCHITECTURE.md in the project.

      Built with

      • Claude API (Anthropic)Reads the food in each photo, matched against the restaurant's menu, and, when AI reasoning is on, writes each agent's proposals, objections and votes. Safety rules and vote counting stay in plain code.
      • ElevenLabsFlash v2.5 gives Speed, Cost, Need, the dispatcher and the shelter their own voices. Scribe turns a shelter's spoken answer into text the agents act on.
      • Cisco Outshift challengeBuilt for "When AI Agents Disagree", Level 3: agents with competing goals, no central decision-maker, and clear rules for when to stop, keep disagreeing, or ask a person.
      • NVIDIA Brev (optional)The photo reader can also run on an open vision model (Qwen2.5-VL) on a Brev GPU instead of Claude. See docs/BREV_SETUP.md.
